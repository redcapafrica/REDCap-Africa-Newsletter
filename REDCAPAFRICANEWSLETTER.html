User Spotlight

Naomi Njenga
African Population and Health Research Center, Kenya

I currently work within a pool of data managers at MRC Unit the Gambia @ LSHTM, where we support research projects both within the Unit and across the sub-region. I serve as the focal Data Manager for the ALERRT consortium under the supervision of our Head of Department, Bai Lamin Dondeh. Within the ALERRT consortium, I contribute to Work Package 3 (WP3), which focuses on developing robust data systems, ensuring data quality and standardization, and supporting secure, multi-country data collection and sharing. Over the years, we have leveraged Redcap and its functionalities to support a range of projects, including the FISSA Study, Long COVID, CCP, the ACE2 Polymorphism Study, and the ongoing SeroMARV study. The ACE2 Polymorphism Study was a multi-site project conducted in Uganda, Cameroon, and Senegal, exploring genetic and immunological factors influencing COVID-19 outcomes. I designed and hosted the Redcap database, supporting both prospective and retrospective data collection, coordinating across sites, and managing data cleaning and query resolution processes. Currently, I am supporting the SeroMARV study, a multi-country research project implemented in Guinea, Cameroon, and Uganda. The study aims to understand the epidemiology and transmission dynamics of the Marburg virus by assessing seroprevalence and identifying potential risk factors associated with exposure. It involves the collection and analysis of demographic, clinical, and laboratory data to inform public health interventions, outbreak preparedness, and response strategies, while also strengthening surveillance systems and research capacity in participating countries. In this project, I lead the design of the Redcap database, develop data management tools, and ensure data quality and consistency across all sites throughout the study lifecycle. Working on multi-country studies presents several challenges, including language barriers, data harmonization, and real-time data resolution. However, Redcap's multi-language functionality has been instrumental in bridging language gaps between our French- and English-speaking sites. I have also leveraged the Data Access Group (DAG) functionality to manage a single centralized Redcap project for the SeroMARV study, allowing one project to share resources across multiple sites. This enables us to monitor data from different sites in real time, supports data harmonization, and ensures that data remain uniform, consistent, and interoperable. Additionally, I have utilized the inbuilt Redcap dashboard to track participant enrolment by site and total household enrolment by country, as well as a query management matrix to monitor query status (open and closed) and average resolution time. These tools have significantly improved reporting and supported sites in monitoring queries and enrolment in real time. Beyond Redcap, I have explored the Redcap API to integrate with tools such as Power BI, Python, and R Shiny for advanced data visualization and real-time dashboards. This has enhanced our ability to generate insights and expand Redcap's functionality within our projects. Working with the API has also provided a practical learning experience, allowing me to explore integrating Redcap with other applications. Finally, Redcap has been instrumental in streamlining multi-country studies, improving data quality, and enabling real-time monitoring. I look forward to continuing to leverage its capabilities to support impactful research across low- and middle-income countries.
