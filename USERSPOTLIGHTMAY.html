User Spotlight

Aneesa Lakhi
The Aurum Institute – Klerksdorp Clinical Research Site

My journey with REDCap began in 2023, at a time when our clinical research site was juggling multiple trials, growing documentation demands, and the daily reality of managing participant flow in a busy clinic environment. What started as a simple idea to improve clinic tracking ended up fundamentally changing the way we work—and how I personally engage with data, systems, and innovation in clinical research. Our Principal Investigator initially developed a REDCap-based visit tracker to map out a participant’s journey through the clinic on any given visit day. The tracker captured every station a participant passed through—starting at reception and administration, through informed consent, counselling, clinical assessments, pharmacy for investigational product dispensation, reimbursement, quality review, and finally data capture. The idea was powerful. In theory, it allowed us to see exactly where a participant was at any moment, what had already been done, what was still outstanding, and how long different types of visits took. This gave us valuable insights into clinic flow, bottlenecks, and participant waiting times. However, implementation came with challenges. The research team felt that the tracker duplicated what they were already documenting manually in participant binders and that it did not fully align with how the clinic flowed in practice.

This feedback became a turning point. Rather than abandoning REDCap, our PI took a bold step—designing an entire clinical trial database and moving the study to fully digital source documentation. For the team, myself included, this was an exciting transition. Aside from a few teething problems, adoption was surprisingly smooth. The shift significantly reduced delays caused by file movement, allowed for real-time review of processes, and improved oversight of how long participants spent in the clinic. My direct involvement with REDCap deepened further when I worked on designing an electronic informed consent (e-Consent) database for our next clinical trial, in collaboration with the study sponsor. This was a particularly exciting avenue for me, as it required thinking beyond traditional data capture—considering participant understanding, ethics, usability, and regulatory requirements within a digital framework. This experience led to my acceptance at the REDCap Africa Symposium 2024, where I presented an oral abstract titled “Embracing Electronic Consent: A Modern Alternative to Paper-based Consent in Clinical Trials.” Sharing our work at a continental platform reinforced how site-level innovation can contribute meaningfully to broader research conversations.

Currently, I am involved in a project driving innovative learning and engagement strategies through gamification and experiential methodologies across diverse stakeholder groups. For this project, I designed REDCap data collection tools for baseline and end-line surveys, as well as focus group discussions. This work has highlighted REDCap’s flexibility beyond traditional clinical trials, particularly for educational and community-focused research. What I value most about REDCap is its adaptability. Whether supporting clinic flow tracking, digital source documentation, electronic consent, or qualitative research, REDCap provides a platform that evolves with the needs of the research team. While my experience with REDCap has been largely positive, there have also been some real challenges along the way. One of the most significant has been sponsor reluctance to use REDCap—even when it is proposed purely as a site-developed electronic source (e-source) system. Despite REDCap’s flexibility and strong global adoption, there often seems to be a lack of understanding or confidence in its use for source documentation at the clinical research site level. Internally, we have taken meaningful steps to address this. As an organisation, we have worked to validate our internal REDCap server and application against 21 CFR Part 11 requirements. This process has strengthened our internal confidence in the system and demonstrated our commitment to compliance, data integrity, and participant protection. However, we are still missing a formal external evaluator, largely due to funding constraints, which has limited how far we can take these discussions with sponsors.

Another ongoing challenge is competition from other proprietary database systems that sponsors may already favour or be contractually aligned with. In some cases, these systems are selected by default, even when REDCap could meet the study needs just as effectively—if not more flexibly—especially from a site perspective. These challenges have highlighted the importance of advocacy, education, and collaboration. I am hopeful that with continued support from the REDCap Africa Consortium, we can strengthen the case for REDCap as a robust, compliant, and sustainable solution. I also hope this collective experience will help us engage internal senior management and external stakeholders more effectively, opening the door for wider acceptance of REDCap-driven e-source solutions in the future. If there is one insight I would share with other REDCap users, it is this: start with the problem you are trying to solve, listen to your end users, and allow your projects to evolve. Innovation often comes from refining what doesn’t initially work, rather than aiming for perfection from the start. REDCap has become more than a data capture tool in my work—it has been a catalyst for improved workflows, collaboration, and continuous learning.
