User Spotlight

Irma Maré
Vanderbilt University Medical Centre, United States of America

When I first walked in Charlotte Maxeke Academic Hospital in Johannesburg as a clinical research assistant at the University of the Witwatersrand, I would never in a million years have guessed at the surprising path my life would follow over the next fifteen years. Through a love of problem solving, possessing more curiosity than is strictly healthy, and a great deal of luck, my work with managing research data for the department of Radiation Oncology brought me into contact with REDCap, the software that would become a central theme in my life. Working with Dr Michael Klipin and Professor Scott Hazelhurst, two of the many extraordinary individuals who would play a part in shaping the arc of my career and me as a person, we brought online an instance of REDCap to support the Faculty of Health Sciences' mission of making electronic capture of research data easier and more secure. Within a couple of months, I went from being the first user to start tinkering with REDCap, to being the one who helped other researchers get their projects started on the system, and by the end of the first year, we had 140 end-users and REDCap Administrator was my full-time role.

Every day brought interesting new projects with intriguing problems to solve, remarkable researchers to engage with and the opportunity to build, expand and grow. One of my most successful support programs was the development of hands-on training workshops for end-users which helped to bridge a lot of the technology hesitancy that was prevalent in my environment. It also gave me the opportunity to witness the delight of new users discovering REDCap for the first time, and realizing just how powerful but also accessible the tool was. And that, I think, reveals one of the great secrets of REDCap and the REDCap Consortium - at its core, it seeks to empower. As I attended my first REDCapCon in Portland, Oregon in 2015, I realized that REDCap attracts the type of person who likes to do, solve and build. And when those types of people are brought together, and the things they do and solve start to build upon each other, that is when the magic happens.

Kindled with a new vision of bringing some of that REDCapCon magic back to my own community in South Africa, I started working on what would become the first REDCap Africa symposium. Joined by my singular sidekick Mapule Nhlapho, we put together an academic meeting hosted at Wits University that was bolstered by the attendance and star power of Professor Paul Harris of Vanderbilt Health in Nashville, Tenessee, who is the inventor of REDCap. And sure enough, the magic of REDCap now grew beyond our own organization, bringing us together with Katherine Johnston of the University of Cape Town, Brendan Gilbert and Jaco Dreyer of Africa Health Research Institute. When my life circumstances led me to seek out new horizons to explore, Mapule stepped up to take on the burden and joy of managing REDCap at the University of the Witwatersrand. I am truly humbled by what she, and the other members of the REDCap Africa Committee, have achieved, and deeply grateful that I have been a part of something that continues to be meaningful in people's lives.

Now working directly with the wonderful teams at Vanderbilt Health responsible for maintaining REDCap and ever expanding its features, reach and impact, I am still discovering interesting challenges to overcome and new ways to make a difference. But through this vast network of wonderful, inspiring and brilliant individuals, I still feel the tug of interconnectedness that leads right back to my home-people. In conclusion, I'd like to leave you with this message - it is easy to lose hope in the face of tumultuous times, but if you are able to stay curious, keep your heart and your mind open, and every day leave the world just a little better than you found it, one day the magic will find you too.
