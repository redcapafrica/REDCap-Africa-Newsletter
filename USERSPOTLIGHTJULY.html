User Spotlight

Montserrat Barrios
Doctors Without Borders, Mozambique

My REDCap Journey: From the Field to the Data Dashboard. My path to mastering REDCap started directly in the field. Working as an experienced field worker with Doctors Without Borders (MSF), I witnessed firsthand the critical importance of accurate data collection in challenging, fast-paced environments. Transitioning into my current role as a junior researcher, I quickly realized that REDCap was the perfect bridge between my hands-on field experience and rigorous scientific analysis. When I first started out, the platform felt like a massive ecosystem to navigate. I found that the best way to learn was simply through daily practice. I began treating REDCap like a playground—building random projects purely as exercises, exploring how to collect aggregate data, and testing out different configurations. That daily interaction helped me build my confidence little by little. Today, I leverage that combined fieldwork and research background to manage complex, individual-level data for vital health initiatives, including various MSF projects.

In my day-to-day role, I manage both research and operational workflows. On the research side, my specialty has become building and designing complex, longitudinal projects from scratch. Currently, I am deeply involved in: The CABLA Project- A single-center longitudinal study implemented by Beira project of MSF, The HALO Project-A sprawling, multicentric study where I did volunteer for the project design on Redcap, The AmbiOne Project- A collaborative effort where I handle essential quality control (QC) in partnership with Wits University. Outside of formal research, my fieldwork background comes in handy when managing rapid public health datasets. I have used REDCap to oversee operational data tracking for major initiatives, such as the Palma testing campaign data and the Pemba Test and Treat campaign. Whether it is a structured clinical trial or an urgent humanitarian response campaign, REDCap handles the data workflows seamlessly.

As someone managing data from the ground up, it comes down to a few game-changing features: Longitudinal Setups- The ability to track a single participant across multiple events and easily repeat instruments has transformed how we trace long-term patient history, The RStudio API- Connecting REDCap directly to RStudio via the API has completely streamlined my data cleaning and analysis pipelines, external modules & open knowledge- The vast library of modules allows for incredible customization, and the open-source nature of REDCap resources means there is always a guide available when you get stuck, The Global Community- Knowing there is a massive, helpful worldwide community facing the same challenges makes the learning curve so much easier to climb.

If there is one piece of advice I can share with fellow junior researchers or field staff who might feel overwhelmed, it is to embrace the slow build. Daily practice is the secret ingredient. You don't need to know everything on day one; you just need to keep clicking, building, and testing. As for my next steps? My ultimate goal is to transition into a REDCap Administrator role. I want access to the full suite of backend tools so I can combine my field insights with administrative power, letting my creativity run wild with project design—and maybe finally see what happens behind the curtain! Hahaha. Acknowledgements to Doctors without borders at Mozambique mission, Beira project and SA medical unit, specially for development support during my journey!. As well as Redcap community in SA who encourage me after every mistake, to try better.
